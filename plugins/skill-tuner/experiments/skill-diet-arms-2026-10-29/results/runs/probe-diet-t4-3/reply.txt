I wrote `./biggest.sh` and ran it once. It prints the three largest files under `./data`, largest first, as `size path` with sizes in bytes:

```
12000 ./data/b.txt
8000 ./data/d.dat
5000 ./data/a.bin
```

The script uses `find ./data -type f -printf '%s %p\n' | sort -rn | head -n 3`. It relies on GNU `find`, which is what this Linux environment has.
