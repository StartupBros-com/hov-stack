I wrote `./biggest.sh` and ran it. It prints each file's size in bytes, then its path, largest first:

```
12000 ./data/b.txt
8000 ./data/d.dat
5000 ./data/a.bin
```

The script runs `find ./data -type f -printf '%s %p\n'`, sorts the results numerically in descending order, and keeps the top three. It searches subdirectories too, not just the top level of `./data`.
