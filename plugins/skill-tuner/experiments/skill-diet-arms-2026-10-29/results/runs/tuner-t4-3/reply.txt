I wrote `./biggest.sh` and ran it once. It prints the three largest files under `./data` with sizes in bytes, largest first:

```
12000 ./data/b.txt
8000 ./data/d.dat
5000 ./data/a.bin
```

The script is a single pipeline: `find ./data -type f -printf '%s %p\n' | sort -rn | head -n 3`. It relies on GNU `find` for `-printf`.
