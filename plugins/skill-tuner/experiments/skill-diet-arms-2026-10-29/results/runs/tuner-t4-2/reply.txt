I wrote `./biggest.sh` and ran it once. It works. Its output was:

```
12000 ./data/b.txt
8000 ./data/d.dat
5000 ./data/a.bin
```

The script uses `find ./data -type f -printf '%s %p\n' | sort -rn | head -n 3`. That prints each file's size in bytes and its path, sorts by size descending, and keeps the top three.

I didn't check the sizes against `ls -l`.
